# HR policy eval, 2026-10-12

The 50-question set in `eval/hr_policy_questions.json` (40 questions the documents answer, each with an expected document, page and key facts; 10 they do not cover, 6 of them near misses), run through the full pipeline and scored by `evaluation.py` scoring version 2 (claim-to-passage support, `grounding_check.py`).

## Setup

- Answer, grounding-pass and judge model: `gemini-2.5-flash`; embeddings `gemini-embedding-001`. Gemini API free tier; no paid model was used, cost $0.
- Index: 1211 child chunks, 788 parent sections from the 65 HR documents, rebuilt in a scratch copy on 9 Oct 2026 after the original Chroma index crashed (the README's 1,214 is the earlier build). Pipeline settings as in `config.py` defaults (version `v4_fast_compare_grounded`).
- Every answer was generated fresh: the answer cache was emptied before each run, and the report counts cache hits below.
- Calls were paced (one Gemini call every 13 s or more, with backoff on rate-limit replies) to stay inside free-tier limits. The pacer's sleeps are subtracted from the stage timings, so the latency figures and the latency score are the pipeline's own time (raw timings are kept in the JSON as `*_raw`).
- The LLM judge is the same Gemini model that wrote the answers (a separate prompt). One run per mode; no repeat runs, so there is no run-to-run variance estimate.
- The questions were written from the documents, not by HR staff; the expected facts were checked against the extracted page text.

## Notes

- **The OFF run is incomplete.** It ran after the ON run. From hr45 on (about 16:35 UTC), every answer call returned `403 PermissionDenied: Your project has been denied access. Please contact support.`, which is not a rate-limit or billing reply. No further calls were made, so hr45 to hr50 (all six are uncovered questions) have no OFF result. All 40 covered questions completed in both modes; the middle column restricts ON to the same 44 questions.
- **ON and OFF differ in more than the grounding pass.** Each mode generated its own draft answer, and generation is not deterministic, so per-question differences mix the grounding pass with sampling variation. Retrieval is deterministic: both modes cited the expected page on the same 36 of 40 questions.
- **The grounding pass can remove correct facts.** On hr20 the draft gave the correct 12-month notice period; the grounding pass judged it unsupported and turned the answer into a decline. On hr35 it turned a partial answer into a decline.
- **The fact check is strict.** The expected fact "at least 12 months" is not credited for an answer that says "12 months in advance", because "least" is missing (hr20, OFF). Other correct answers that paraphrase are not credited either: "death ... within 8 hours" for "fatalities within 8 hours" (hr04), "labor organizations" for "labor unions" (hr07), "does not have to be made in writing" for "do not need to be in writing" (hr11). Facts-found figures are a lower bound; no answer was regraded by hand.
- **Mean latency with the grounding pass is skewed by one question.** hr16 took 530 s, almost all of it outside the answer and grounding calls (most likely the query-embedding request stalling); the median is the better figure.
- Most covered questions the system declined (hr14 to hr20) are UW faculty and academic staff rules where the expected page was retrieved but the selected passages did not contain the specific detail.

## What the measures mean

- **Claim supported**: the answer is split into sentences and bullets (the confidence line, headings and fragments under 3 content words are skipped). A claim is supported when one chunk the answer was given contains at least 60% of its content words (stopwords removed, crude stemming) and every number in it. The check is lexical: it can mark a correct paraphrase unsupported and cannot catch a claim that reuses a chunk's words with the meaning reversed. Refusals are excluded from the pooled claim count.
- **Expected page among cited chunks**: a chunk given to the model comes from the expected document and its page range overlaps an expected page, or matches an `also_in` alternative.
- **Expected facts found**: every content word and number of the expected fact appears somewhere in the answer.
- **Answered / declined**: whether the answer matches the refusal patterns in `grounding_check.py` (e.g. "do not contain", "insufficient information"). A partial answer that says one detail is missing can count as a refusal.
- **Factual grounding (1-5)** bands the claim support rate (5 at 95%+, 4 at 80%+, 3 at 60%+, 2 at 40%+); a refusal scores 5 on an uncovered question and 1 on a covered one. **Citation quality (1-5)** bands the share of cited chunks that support a claim, capped at 2 when no cited chunk is the expected source.
- **LLM judge**: the same Gemini model rates faithfulness to the retrieved context and relevance, 1-5.
